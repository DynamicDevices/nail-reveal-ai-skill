# Generative design: what the recorded study taught us

The [study package](../../../study/README.md) retains the actual outcomes,
recreation recipes, video derivatives and accepted/rejected comparisons.
It has20 ideas with five accepted-change records each. Many steps constructed
unfinished motifs; the count does not mean100 independently optimised finished
designs. Beauty remains the primary acceptance condition. The abstract butterfly
and weakly recognisable koi show that incremental improvement can still end
below the intended quality bar. Five selected full-hand sets remain unfinished.

## Design for the brush we actually have

Use a few bold curves, large simple shapes, nested insets and deliberate negative
space. No verified fine brush or selective top-colour eraser was available.
Requested longer holds increased density more than size in the earlier contact
test; use confined repeated passes for a patchy core, then inspect. Do not assume
a short contact guarantees a tiny mark. The approximate upward120px offset is
layout-specific, not a universal coordinate conversion.

Keep starting ideas genuinely different, not recolourings of one motif. Vary
position, spacing, scale, layer order, density or curve shape with a stated
purpose. Retain a seed where randomness is used. This was constrained,
evolution-inspired iteration with visual selection, not a full genetic algorithm
or an automatic beauty optimiser. The study README records the research sources.

## Clean before adding detail

Inspect the whole nail for old marks, translucent rims, dark halos and unwanted
connections before building a new design. Use a full strip and clean base when
the underlying design cannot be restored reliably. Background-colour repainting
is an opaque masking technique, not transparent erasing. A pale-green recut and
a white undercoat both failed to remove a dark triangle's halo cleanly; rebuilding
with a plum field and green triangle gave a cleaner result. Neither one trial
nor a palette reversal proves a universal opacity rule.

Dense sampling matters at a mask's boundary. Sparse dots can leave scallops;
changing draw order can unintentionally cover adjoining geometry. A confined
black patch restored an isolated failed moon while preserving the planet below,
but that works only when its support is clear of retained details on a known
flat background. Do not use that as a generic restoration shortcut.

## Inspect the actual Hand, not only the closeup

A whole moon in closeup was clipped in the real Hand view. Moving it inward/down
and checking the actual Hand fixed that candidate. There is no verified universal
closeup→Hand projection. Any extension toward a nail edge requires Hand inspection
before acceptance or dependent layers. Inspect tips, rims, joints, contrast and
the composition as well as the new mark.

Flat sketches can catch collisions, broken orbit tips and layer-order mistakes.
They did not model transparency, shading, brush accumulation, nail clipping or
the actual Hand projection. They are planning aids, not game evidence.

## Reject, restore, then try again

Keep every physically executed failure's recipe, image, video and assessment.
State why a new candidate is visibly better and check for regressions; transport
success or a score is not acceptance. If it is not better, restore the actual
previous best, capture that restoration and make a substantively different try.
Changing a "best" pointer does not restore the phone. Keep unexecuted proposals,
baseline preparation and cleanup outside the accepted-improvement count.

After the five improvements to an idea, record the Lamp/glaze transition and
capture its finished nail. Do not glaze after every intermediate change when
following this study's protocol. Game-generated finish sparkles are not painted
stars. Do not call a two-nail client a complete hand or promise access to untouched
nails merely because they are visible. See [client limits](controls.md).

## Keep speedups optional and evidence honest

The study used finite fresh-ADB batches after persistent-shell failures. It did
not repeatedly relaunch an unchanged broken speedup. No Node daemon or new paid
generator was used. Original PNGs and replayable JSON were retained; crop/scale
maps separate physical input coordinates from inspection previews. The local
media exporter uses an existing Pillow installation and ffmpeg; neither is a
requirement for playing the game with the portable baseline.

Do not infer a measured speedup from shorter scripts, fewer images or one faster
cleanup. Keep timings tied to the actual workload and distinguish input/capture
time from inspection and decision time. Follow the [fallback ladder](optimisation.md#fallback-ladder)
when a speedup fails, and inspect partial effects before resuming.
